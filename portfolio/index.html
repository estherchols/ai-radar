How I workThe same loop I used on deals

The loop I ran on every deal is the loop I run on every product. Only the last step changed.

  1. Ask the question nobody asked

    In a founder meeting it was the question they hadn't rehearsed. In my RDI study it was why AI tools that executives bought never reached the people doing the work.

  2. Write down what would prove me wrong

    A deal memo is a bet with a kill condition. So is a product spec. I decide what the evidence has to show before I go looking.

  3. Go find the part that hurts

    Across 30+ cross-border deals, the story that mattered was rarely in the pitch deck. I look hardest at whatever weakens the case.

  4. Then build it

    As an investor, an idea that survived got a check. Now it gets a prototype.

Field research · Stanford RDI

Why AI stalls inside companies

Companies keep buying AI and the people doing the work keep not using it. I wanted to know where it breaks.

What I did
About 40 interviews with people building and deploying AI inside companies, including Databricks teams, vertical SaaS founders and a16z-backed startups. I built agents on a VPS to run the outreach, scheduling and notes.
What I found
AI enters from the top, through the CEO or a vendor selling to the C-suite, and stalls with the staff. Nobody I spoke to had a working fix.
Where it led
You can't plug AI into an organization. You either adapt the AI to the people or redesign the organization around it, and the real gains come from the redesign. I'm now writing the implementation playbook for that.

AI 101From zero, in plain English

No math and no jargon. Six short lessons that take you from "what is AI" to how agents work.

01What AI actually isPatterns, not rules

Normal software follows rules a person wrote. AI learns patterns from examples instead. Show it a million photos of cats and it learns what a cat looks like without anyone defining one.

Think of a new analyst who reads every past deal memo until they can spot a good deal on their own.

02How a chatbot writesOne word at a time

A large language model reads a huge amount of text and learns to predict the next word. It writes by making that guess over and over. That is why it can sound confident and still be wrong.

It is fluent autocomplete trained on most of the internet.

03Prompts and contextIt only knows what you tell it

The model has no memory of your company, your goals or your last conversation unless you put them in front of it. Better context beats clever wording almost every time.

Brief it like a smart intern on day one.

04From chatbot to agentWhen AI starts taking actions

Give a model tools, like web search, a calendar or the ability to run code, and a goal. Now it can plan steps, use the tools and check its own work. That is an agent.

A chatbot drafts the email. An agent finds the time, writes the invite and sends it.

05Why AI costs what it costsChips, power and money

Every answer runs on specialized chips in data centers that draw enormous amounts of electricity. Compute, capital and regulation set the limits on what gets built, which is what I track on AI Radar.

The bottleneck is rarely the idea. It is who controls the scarce input.

06Where AI breaksWrong answers and empty seats

Models make things up when they lack context. The bigger failure is human. In my research, companies bought AI at the top and the people doing the work never used it.

Good AI products are designed around how people actually work.
